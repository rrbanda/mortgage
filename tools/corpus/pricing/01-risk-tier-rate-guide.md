# Small Business Loan Risk Tier and Interest Rate Guide

## Source: SBA SOP 50 10 v8.1; WSJ Prime Rate Reference; FDIC Community Bank Survey 2024

---

## Risk Tier Definitions

Risk tiers are assigned based on a composite assessment of financial strength, collateral quality, industry risk, and operating history. Each tier corresponds to a range of interest rates and determines the pricing presented to the applicant. Tier assignment should be documented in the underwriting file with specific support for each factor.

### Tier 1 — Low Risk

A Tier 1 applicant demonstrates strong financial performance across all key metrics with no material risk flags.

**Qualifying criteria:**
- Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR): ≥ 2.0x (net operating income is at least twice the annual debt service)
- Loan-to-Value (LTV): ≤ 50% (collateral covers at least twice the loan amount)
- Operating history: 5 or more continuous years in the same business under the same ownership
- Business credit score: 740+ (personal) or equivalent business credit score (Paydex 80+, Experian 76+)
- Industry: Tier 1 low-risk industry (professional services, healthcare, established manufacturing)
- Existing debt: minimal; existing debt-to-revenue ratio < 10%
- Collateral quality: fully collateralized with high-quality assets (owner-occupied commercial real estate preferred)

**Loan characteristics**: These applicants represent the bank's best small business credits and are offered the most competitive rates. Full SBA guarantee fee may be partially absorbed by the bank for relationship borrowers.

### Tier 2 — Moderate Risk

A Tier 2 applicant meets all minimum eligibility requirements with solid financials and one or more moderate risk factors.

**Qualifying criteria:**
- DSCR: 1.50x–1.99x
- LTV: 50%–65%
- Operating history: 3–5 years
- Business credit score: 680–739 personal; Paydex 70–79
- Industry: Tier 1 or Tier 2 industry
- Existing debt: manageable; debt-to-revenue ratio 10–25%

**Loan characteristics**: Standard well-qualified small business borrower. Full SBA guarantee fees apply and are typically passed to the borrower.

### Tier 3 — Elevated Risk

A Tier 3 applicant is eligible but carries meaningful risk factors requiring enhanced underwriting documentation and a higher interest rate to compensate for increased default probability.

**Qualifying criteria:**
- DSCR: 1.25x–1.49x (at or near the minimum)
- LTV: 65%–75%
- Operating history: 2–3 years (meets minimum but limited track record)
- Business credit score: 640–679 personal; Paydex 60–69
- Industry: Tier 2 or Tier 3 industry
- Existing debt: elevated; debt-to-revenue ratio 25–40%
- May have one compensating factor deficiency offset by strength in another area

**Loan characteristics**: Loan proceeds must be used for capital expenditure or specific business purpose (not open-ended working capital). More restrictive covenants may apply. Collateral requirements are strictly enforced.

### Tier 4 — High Risk

A Tier 4 applicant meets the absolute minimum eligibility thresholds and may be recommended for REVIEW rather than automatic ELIGIBLE classification. Loan approval requires senior credit officer sign-off.

**Qualifying criteria:**
- DSCR: exactly at the 1.25x minimum
- LTV: approaching but not exceeding 75%
- Operating history: just over 2 years, or 3+ years in a Tier 3 industry
- Business credit score: below 640 personal
- Industry: Tier 3 industry with limited compensating factors
- Existing debt: high; debt-to-revenue ratio approaching 40%
- Multiple risk flags present simultaneously

**Loan characteristics**: Requires documented senior approval. Restrictive loan covenants (quarterly financial reporting, minimum DSCR maintenance). Borrower education about repayment obligations strongly recommended. SBA guarantee provides lender protection but does not eliminate the need for sound underwriting.

## Interest Rate Ranges by Risk Tier

SBA 7(a) loan interest rates are regulated to not exceed the SBA maximum rate, which is expressed as a spread over the Wall Street Journal Prime Rate (WSJ Prime). As of 2024–2025, WSJ Prime is approximately 8.50%; rates below are expressed both as spreads and approximate APRs at that Prime level.

| Tier | Risk Level | Typical APR Range | SBA Maximum Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Low Risk | 6.00% – 7.00% | WSJ Prime + 2.25% (for loans > 7 yr, fixed) | Best applicants; full collateral; bank may negotiate below maximum |
| Tier 2 | Moderate Risk | 7.50% – 8.75% | WSJ Prime + 2.75% (variable) | Standard pricing; most qualified SBA applicants land here |
| Tier 3 | Elevated Risk | 9.00% – 10.50% | WSJ Prime + 4.75% (variable) | Multiple risk flags; higher rate compensates for default probability |
| Tier 4 | High Risk | 10.50% – 12.00% | WSJ Prime + 6.50% (short term) | Near-boundary eligibility; requires senior approval |

**SBA maximum rate limits by loan maturity (WSJ Prime-based):**
- Loans with maturity ≤ 7 years: Prime + 2.25% (fixed) or Prime + 2.50% (variable)
- Loans with maturity > 7 years: Prime + 2.75% (fixed) or Prime + 3.75% (variable)
- Loans ≤ $50,000: Prime + 6.50% maximum permitted
- SBA Express loans: Prime + 6.50% maximum

## What Drives Tier Assignment

**Years in business** is the single most predictive factor for SBA loan default. Businesses under 2 years fail at 3–4x the rate of businesses with 5+ years of history. A 2-year-old business in a stable industry may warrant Tier 2; a 2-year-old business in event planning warrants Tier 3 or REVIEW.

**Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)** measures whether the business generates enough cash flow to pay the loan. A DSCR of 1.25x means for every $1.00 of debt payment, the business earns $1.25 of net operating income — a 25% cushion. DSCR below 1.25x means the business cannot reliably service the debt from operations alone.

**Loan-to-Value (LTV)** measures collateral coverage. Higher LTV means thinner collateral protection for the lender if the borrower defaults. LTV above 75% leaves the lender insufficiently protected after collection costs and collateral liquidation discounts.

**Industry risk** affects both revenue stability (ability to service debt) and collateral quality (whether the business can retain value in a distressed sale). Event planning businesses in distress liquidate at near-zero value; manufacturing businesses with owned equipment liquidate at meaningful values.

**Credit score** indicates the borrower's historical willingness and ability to service obligations. For small business loans, personal credit of the business owner (particularly for businesses under 5 years) is highly predictive of SBA loan performance.

**Collateral quality** affects recovery in the event of default. Owned commercial real estate is the highest-quality collateral. Accounts receivable, inventory, and leasehold improvements carry high liquidation risk.

**Existing debt load** (debt-to-revenue ratio) indicates how stretched the business's cash flow already is before adding the new loan obligation.

## How to Explain Pricing to Applicants

Applicants should receive a clear, professional explanation of their interest rate without requiring disclosure of internal risk scoring details. The following templates may be used:

**Tier 1 explanation**: "Based on your strong operating history and financial performance, we are pleased to offer you a competitive interest rate of [X%]. This reflects our confidence in your business's ability to service this loan."

**Tier 2 explanation**: "Your application reflects solid financial fundamentals. The interest rate of [X%] reflects our standard pricing for businesses at your stage and in your industry. This rate is consistent with SBA program guidelines for well-qualified applicants."

**Tier 3 explanation**: "Your business qualifies for financing. The rate of [X%] reflects a combination of factors including your industry classification and operating history. As your business continues to build its track record, refinancing at a lower rate may be possible in 2–3 years."

**Tier 4 explanation**: "Your business meets our eligibility requirements. Given the current financial profile of your business, the interest rate of [X%] reflects additional risk factors that our underwriting team has documented. We want to ensure your business can comfortably service this loan — our loan officer can walk through the repayment schedule with you."

**Do not** disclose specific risk tier designations, DSCR calculations, or internal scoring thresholds to applicants. These are proprietary underwriting criteria.

## SBA Guarantee Fee Schedule

The SBA charges a guarantee fee to the lender for each guaranteed loan. This fee is typically passed through to the borrower and added to the loan cost. Lenders must disclose the guarantee fee as part of the loan's total cost in the SBA authorization document.

| Loan Amount | SBA Guarantee | Guarantee Fee Rate | Example (on guaranteed portion) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to $150,000 | 85% | 0% (fee waived) | $0 |
| $150,001 – $700,000 | 75% | 3.00% of guaranteed portion | $700K loan: 75% × $700K × 3% = $15,750 |
| $700,001 – $5,000,000 (first $1M) | 75% | 3.50% on first $1M of guaranteed portion | $1M guaranteed: 3.5% × $750K = $26,250 |
| $700,001 – $5,000,000 (above $1M) | 75% | 3.75% on guaranteed portion above $1M | $2M guaranteed (above $1M): 3.75% × $1.5M = $56,250 |

**Annual service fee**: SBA also charges an ongoing annual service fee of 0.55% of the outstanding guaranteed balance, collected annually.

**Fee waivers**: Veteran-owned businesses (SBA Veterans Advantage program) receive a 0% upfront guarantee fee on loans up to $500,000. Some SBA-designated programs for underserved markets may also offer fee reductions.

Guarantee fees are tax-deductible as a business expense for the borrower in the year paid.
