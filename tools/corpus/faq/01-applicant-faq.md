# Small Business Loan Applicant FAQ

## Source: Cymbal Bank Small Business Lending Team; SBA 7(a) Program Guidelines

---

**Q1: What loan programs are available for small businesses?**

Cymbal Bank offers SBA 7(a) loans as the primary small business loan product, which is the SBA's most flexible and widely used program. The standard SBA 7(a) loan supports working capital, equipment purchases, real estate acquisition, and business acquisitions up to $5 million. For smaller needs, the SBA Express program (up to $500,000) offers faster approval timelines. Businesses that do not qualify for the standard program may be referred to SBA Microloan (up to $50,000) or SBA Community Advantage programs.

**Q2: What is the minimum operating history required?**

The standard SBA 7(a) loan requires a minimum of 2 years of continuous operating history under the same ownership. This means the business must have been actively generating revenue for at least 24 months prior to the application date. The 2-year requirement reflects SBA policy that new businesses have significantly higher default rates than established businesses.

**Q3: My business is less than 2 years old — can I still apply?**

Businesses with less than 2 years of operating history are generally not eligible for the standard SBA 7(a) loan. Businesses with 12–23 months of history may qualify for the SBA Microloan program (up to $50,000, through SBA-designated nonprofit intermediaries) or SBA Community Advantage loans, which have more flexible underwriting for startups and underserved markets. Businesses under 12 months of age have very limited financing options through SBA programs.

**Q4: What credit score do I need to qualify?**

There is no single minimum credit score that guarantees approval, but as a general guideline, a personal credit score of 640 or above for the primary business owner is typically required for SBA 7(a) consideration. Scores above 680 improve eligibility significantly, and scores above 740 qualify for the most competitive pricing. The SBA also uses the Small Business Scoring Service (SBSS), a business credit score; a minimum SBSS score of 155 is required for expedited processing on loans up to $500,000.

**Q5: How much can I borrow?**

The maximum loan amount under the SBA 7(a) program is $5,000,000. The actual amount approved depends on the business's verified annual revenue, debt service coverage ratio, collateral available, and the specific loan purpose. As a general rule, approved loan amounts typically do not exceed 50% of the business's trailing 12-month gross annual revenue. A business with $500,000 in annual revenue would typically qualify for up to $250,000.

**Q6: What is the maximum loan-to-revenue ratio?**

Cymbal Bank's standard policy limits the loan amount to no more than 50% of the applicant's verified trailing 12-month gross annual revenue. Loan amounts between 50% and 75% of annual revenue are reviewed with additional scrutiny and require strong compensating factors (full collateral, DSCR above 1.5x, long operating history). Loan amounts exceeding 75% of annual revenue are generally classified as INELIGIBLE because the debt burden would be unsustainable relative to the business's revenue base.

**Q7: What collateral is required?**

Collateral is required for all loans over $50,000. For loans between $50,000 and $500,000, the lender must take all available business assets as collateral (equipment, inventory, accounts receivable) and may also require personal real estate if business assets are insufficient to cover the loan amount. For loans over $500,000, full collateralization is required to the extent possible. The most valuable forms of collateral are owner-occupied commercial real estate, equipment, and eligible accounts receivable.

**Q8: What if I don't have collateral to offer?**

The SBA's policy is that a loan should not be declined solely because it cannot be fully collateralized, provided the borrower otherwise meets all eligibility and credit standards. However, in practice, loans without meaningful collateral face significant additional scrutiny and typically require compensating strengths in all other areas: DSCR well above 1.5x, very strong operating history (5+ years), and excellent credit. Unsecured or under-collateralized loans are more likely to receive a REVIEW recommendation requiring manual credit officer approval.

**Q9: What industries are not eligible for SBA loans?**

Several industry categories are explicitly prohibited from receiving SBA loans under 13 CFR § 120.110. These include: non-profit organizations; financial businesses primarily engaged in lending (banks, finance companies); passive real estate landlords; life insurance companies; businesses engaged in illegal activities under federal law (including cannabis); gambling businesses deriving more than one-third of revenue from gambling; and businesses whose associates have felony convictions or active indictments. A full list is available in the SBA SOP 50 10.

**Q10: My business is in event planning — am I eligible?**

Event planning businesses (NAICS 561920, 711310, 711320) are eligible for SBA loans but are classified as Tier 3 Elevated Risk industries due to seasonal revenue concentration, limited hard collateral, and sensitivity to economic disruptions. To qualify, event planning businesses typically need at least 3 years of operating history, a Debt Service Coverage Ratio of at least 1.5x, and meaningful collateral. Businesses with less than 2 years of operating history in event planning are ineligible under the standard SBA operating history requirement.

**Q11: What does DSCR mean and how is it calculated?**

DSCR stands for Debt Service Coverage Ratio. It measures whether your business generates enough income to cover its loan payments. The formula is: DSCR = Net Operating Income ÷ Total Annual Debt Service (all existing loan payments plus the new proposed loan payment). A DSCR of 1.25 means your business earns $1.25 for every $1.00 of debt payment — a 25% cushion. The SBA requires a minimum DSCR of 1.25x; higher-risk applications require 1.5x or above.

**Q12: What is an adverse action notice?**

An adverse action notice is a written document required by the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) and Regulation B (12 CFR Part 1002) that must be provided to any loan applicant whose application is denied, whose credit terms are made less favorable than requested, or whose existing account is terminated or downgraded. The notice must be provided within 30 days of the decision and must include the specific reasons for the adverse action. You have the right to request specific reasons if they are not included in the initial notice.

**Q13: What specific reasons will be given if my loan is declined?**

If your loan is declined, you are entitled under Regulation B to a statement of specific reasons for the adverse action — general statements like "you didn't meet our standards" are not sufficient. Common specific decline reasons include: insufficient operating history, loan-to-revenue ratio exceeds policy, inadequate collateral, debt service coverage ratio below minimum, industry ineligibility under SBA regulations, derogatory credit history, unable to verify business revenue, and excessive existing debt obligations.

**Q14: Can I reapply after being declined?**

Yes. There is no SBA-mandated waiting period before reapplication. However, reapplying immediately without addressing the specific reasons for decline will result in the same outcome. We recommend reviewing your adverse action notice, addressing each specific reason identified, and reapplying once those issues have been resolved — typically after 6 to 24 months depending on the type of deficiency.

**Q15: How long does the underwriting process take?**

The standard SBA 7(a) underwriting process at Cymbal Bank takes approximately 7–14 business days from receipt of a complete application package. SBA Express loans (up to $500,000) are typically processed within 5–7 business days. Delays occur when documentation is incomplete, when additional information is required during underwriting review, or when the application requires a senior credit officer decision. Providing a complete and accurate application package at the outset significantly reduces processing time.

**Q16: What documents do I need to provide?**

A complete SBA 7(a) loan application requires: (1) SBA Form 1919 (Borrower Information Form); (2) Business tax returns for the past 3 years; (3) Personal tax returns of all owners with 20%+ equity for the past 3 years; (4) Current year-to-date business financial statements (profit & loss statement and balance sheet within 90 days); (5) A/R and A/P aging schedules; (6) Business license and entity formation documents; (7) Bank statements for the past 12 months; (8) Collateral documentation (appraisals, titles); (9) Business debt schedule; (10) A business plan or loan purpose description.

**Q17: What does ELIGIBLE, REVIEW, and INELIGIBLE mean?**

These are the three possible underwriting outcomes from the automated loan analysis. ELIGIBLE means the application meets all policy requirements and can proceed to a final approval decision. REVIEW means the application meets minimum thresholds but has one or more risk factors requiring manual credit officer evaluation — the loan may still be approved after review. INELIGIBLE means the application does not meet one or more mandatory eligibility criteria (such as operating history, loan-to-revenue ratio, industry prohibition, or DSCR minimum) and cannot be approved in its current form.

**Q18: What happens after I receive a REVIEW recommendation?**

A REVIEW recommendation means your application will be escalated to a senior credit officer for manual evaluation. The credit officer will assess whether compensating factors offset the identified risks, may request additional documentation, and may propose modified loan terms (lower amount, shorter term, additional collateral) that would bring the application within eligible parameters. The manual review process typically adds 3–5 business days to the standard timeline.

**Q19: Can I appeal a decline decision?**

Cymbal Bank does not have a formal appeal process for loan decisions that follow SBA guidelines. However, if you believe your application was declined based on incorrect information or that relevant favorable information was not considered, you may request a reconsideration by providing corrected documentation or additional evidence. Reconsideration requests must be submitted in writing to your loan officer within 60 days of the adverse action notice.

**Q20: What is the difference between SBA 7(a) and SBA Express?**

The SBA 7(a) standard loan offers up to $5,000,000 with a government guarantee of up to 85% (for loans ≤ $150,000) or 75% (for larger loans). SBA Express offers up to $500,000 with a 50% guarantee, which allows lenders to use their own underwriting processes and documentation requirements, resulting in faster approval (typically 36 hours from SBA vs. 5–10 business days for standard). The tradeoff is that SBA Express loans carry a higher maximum interest rate (WSJ Prime + 6.50%) compared to standard 7(a) rates. Most well-qualified borrowers benefit from the standard 7(a) program's lower rates and higher guarantee.
