# 12 CFR § 1002.9 — Notifications (ECOA / Regulation B)

## Source: Cornell LII / e-CFR (Title 12, Chapter X, Part 1002)

## Regulatory Text

§ 1002.9 Notifications.

(a) Notification of action taken, ECOA notice, and statement of specific reasons—

(1) When notification is required. A creditor shall notify an applicant of action taken within:
(i) 30 days after receiving a completed application concerning the creditor's approval of, counteroffer to, or adverse action on the application;
(ii) 30 days after taking adverse action on an incomplete application;
(iii) 30 days after taking adverse action on an existing account;
(iv) 90 days after notifying the applicant of a counteroffer if the applicant does not expressly accept or use the credit offered.

(2) Content of notification when adverse action is taken. A notification given to an applicant when adverse action is taken shall be in writing and shall contain:
- A statement of the action taken
- The name and address of the creditor
- A statement of the provisions of section 701(a) of the Act (ECOA notice)
- The name and address of the Federal agency that administers compliance
- Either: (i) A statement of specific reasons for the action taken; OR (ii) A disclosure of the applicant's right to a statement of specific reasons within 30 days, if the statement is requested within 60 days

(3) Notification to business credit applicants:
(i) For businesses with gross revenues of $1 million or less:
   - The statement of action taken may be given orally or in writing when adverse action is taken
   - Disclosure of applicant's right to a statement of reasons may be given at time of application instead of when adverse action is taken
(ii) For businesses with gross revenues exceeding $1 million:
   - Notify the applicant within a reasonable time, orally or in writing, of the action taken
   - Provide a written statement of reasons for adverse action if the applicant makes a written request within 60 days

(b) Form of ECOA notice and statement of specific reasons—

(1) ECOA notice. The Federal Equal Credit Opportunity Act prohibits creditors from discriminating against credit applicants on the basis of race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age (provided the applicant has the capacity to enter into a binding contract); because all or part of the applicant's income derives from any public assistance program; or because the applicant has in good faith exercised any right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act.

(2) Statement of specific reasons. The statement of reasons for adverse action must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s) for the adverse action. Statements that the adverse action was based on the creditor's internal standards or policies or that the applicant did not meet the creditor's minimum standards are insufficient.

---

## Required Elements of an Adverse Action Notice

Every adverse action notice issued to a business credit applicant must contain all of the following elements to be compliant with 12 CFR § 1002.9:

- [ ] **Statement of adverse action**: A clear, unambiguous statement that the application has been denied, that credit has been reduced, or that the account has been terminated
- [ ] **Creditor name and address**: Full legal name and mailing address of the bank or lending institution issuing the notice
- [ ] **ECOA notice**: Verbatim or substantially similar statement of the Equal Credit Opportunity Act prohibition against discrimination based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, public assistance income, or exercise of rights under the Consumer Credit Protection Act
- [ ] **Federal regulatory agency**: Name and contact information of the federal agency that administers ECOA compliance for this creditor (e.g., FDIC, OCC, CFPB, Federal Reserve)
- [ ] **Specific reasons for adverse action**: A statement identifying the principal specific reasons for the adverse action, OR a disclosure of the applicant's right to request those reasons within 60 days
- [ ] **Date of notice**: The date the notice is issued (used to establish the 30-day compliance window)

For business credit applicants with gross revenues exceeding $1 million, written reasons must be provided upon written request within 60 days of the creditor's notification. For businesses with revenues of $1 million or less, the notice must be in writing when adverse action is taken.

## Specific Decline Reason Codes

The following are the principal specific reasons that satisfy the § 1002.9(b)(2) specificity requirement for business loan adverse action notices. Vague statements like "did not meet our standards" are explicitly prohibited by the regulation. Each reason must reflect an actual factor that contributed to the adverse decision.

1. **Derogatory credit history** — The applicant's business or personal credit report reflects past delinquencies, charge-offs, collections, judgments, or bankruptcies that indicate an unacceptable risk of default.

2. **Insufficient income / revenue** — The applicant's documented annual revenue or net operating income is insufficient to support the requested loan amount under the lender's debt service coverage requirements.

3. **Excessive existing obligations** — The applicant carries existing debt (business loans, credit cards, lines of credit) at a level that, when combined with the new loan payment, would result in a Debt Service Coverage Ratio below the minimum required threshold of 1.25x.

4. **Inadequate collateral** — The value of assets offered as collateral, after applying applicable LTV discounts, is insufficient to secure the requested loan amount. Collateral coverage fell below the lender's required minimum.

5. **Insufficient operating history** — The applicant's business has not been operating for the minimum period required by bank policy (typically 2 years for standard SBA 7(a) loans). Businesses with less than 12 months of operating history are generally ineligible.

6. **Loan-to-revenue ratio exceeds policy limit** — The requested loan amount exceeds the maximum permitted percentage of the applicant's verified annual gross revenue. Standard policy limits the loan amount to 50% of trailing 12-month revenue; amounts exceeding 75% of annual revenue trigger INELIGIBLE classification.

7. **Industry ineligible under SBA regulations** — The applicant's industry or business type is listed as ineligible under 13 CFR § 120.110 (e.g., cannabis, gambling in excess of one-third of revenue, financial lending businesses, life insurance carriers, non-profit organizations).

8. **Unable to verify business revenue** — The applicant was unable to provide or the lender was unable to independently verify the stated annual revenue through bank statements, tax returns, or audited financial statements.

9. **Business entity not properly established** — The business is not registered as a legal entity in its state of operation, lacks a valid EIN, or does not have an established business bank account separate from personal accounts.

10. **Insufficient business credit references** — The applicant's business does not have an established business credit profile (DUNS/Paydex, Experian Business, or Equifax Business) with sufficient tradeline history to support the loan amount requested.

11. **Debt service coverage ratio below minimum** — The calculated DSCR (net operating income divided by total annual debt service including the proposed new loan) is below the required minimum of 1.25x, meaning the business does not generate sufficient cash flow to reliably service the debt.

12. **Collateral value insufficient after appraisal** — A professional appraisal of offered collateral (real estate, equipment, or other assets) determined the fair market or orderly liquidation value to be lower than estimated by the applicant, resulting in insufficient coverage.

13. **Business operating history below required minimum** — The business has been in operation for fewer years than required by policy for the requested loan type. SBA 7(a) standard loans require a minimum of 2 years of continuous operating history; businesses with 8–23 months of history may be referred to SBA Microloan or Community Advantage programs.

14. **Prior federal loan default** — The applicant, or a business previously owned or controlled by the applicant, defaulted on a prior federally backed loan (SBA, USDA, FHA, or other federal program), causing a loss to the federal government. This is a mandatory disqualifier under 13 CFR § 120.110(q) absent an SBA waiver.

15. **Criminal history of a business associate** — An owner, officer, or key associate of the applicant business is currently incarcerated, under indictment, on probation, or on parole for a felony or financial crime, making the business ineligible under 13 CFR § 120.110(n).

## Timing Requirements Summary

| Scenario | Notification Deadline | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Completed application — approval, counteroffer, or adverse action | Within 30 days of receiving the completed application | § 1002.9(a)(1)(i) |
| Adverse action on incomplete application | Within 30 days of taking adverse action | § 1002.9(a)(1)(ii) |
| Adverse action on an existing account | Within 30 days of taking adverse action | § 1002.9(a)(1)(iii) |
| Counteroffer not accepted by applicant | Within 90 days of counteroffer notification | § 1002.9(a)(1)(iv) |
| Request for specific reasons (applicant request) | Creditor must provide written reasons within 30 days of applicant's written request, if requested within 60 days of adverse action notice | § 1002.9(a)(2)(ii) |

**Compliance note**: Failure to provide timely and specific adverse action notices is a violation of ECOA and may subject the lender to regulatory examination findings, civil liability under 15 U.S.C. § 1691e (up to $10,000 actual damages, plus punitive damages up to $500,000 in class actions), and reputational harm. All adverse action notices must be retained in the loan file for a minimum of 25 months from the date of the notice (12 CFR § 1002.12).
