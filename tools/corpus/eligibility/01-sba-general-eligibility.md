# SBA 7(a) Loan Program — General Eligibility Requirements
## Source: SBA SOP 50 10 v8.1 (June 2025)

## Business Size Standards

To qualify for an SBA 7(a) loan, the business must qualify as a small business under SBA size standards (13 CFR Part 121). Size is measured by either average annual receipts or number of employees, depending on the industry NAICS code.

- **Retail and service businesses**: generally under $8 million to $41.5 million in average annual receipts
- **Manufacturing businesses**: generally under 500 to 1,500 employees
- **Wholesale trade**: generally under 100 to 250 employees

The business must be independently owned and operated, not dominant in its field, and meet SBA size criteria at the time of application.

## Operating History Requirements

SBA 7(a) loans require the applicant business to have an established operating history:

- **Minimum 2 years of operating history** required for standard 7(a) loans
- Businesses with less than 2 years of history require additional documentation and may qualify for SBA Microloan or SBA Community Advantage programs instead
- Businesses with less than 12 months of operating history are generally ineligible for standard 7(a) loans
- Franchises operating under a recognized franchise agreement may qualify with shorter history if the franchisor meets SBA eligibility standards

## Annual Revenue Requirements

- Business must demonstrate sustainable revenue to support loan repayment
- **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) must be at least 1.25x**: net operating income must cover debt payments by at least 25%
- Lenders assess trailing 12-month and 3-year average revenue trends
- Revenue must be from eligible business operations (not personal income or passive sources)

## Maximum Loan Amounts (SBA 7(a))

- **Standard 7(a)**: up to $5,000,000
- **SBA Express**: up to $500,000 (expedited review, higher interest permitted)
- **Export Express**: up to $500,000 for export-related purposes
- **SBA Veterans Advantage**: fee-reduced loans for eligible veteran-owned businesses

## Eligible Uses of Proceeds

Loan proceeds must be used for legitimate business purposes:

- Working capital (operating expenses, payroll, inventory)
- Equipment purchase or leases
- Real estate purchase or leasehold improvements
- Business acquisition or buyout
- Refinancing of existing business debt (subject to restrictions)
- Export financing

Proceeds may **not** be used to:
- Pay delinquent taxes or personal obligations
- Reimburse owners for equity previously injected
- Finance passive real estate investments
- Fund activities not directly related to the business

## Ownership and Management

- At least 51% of the business must be owned by U.S. citizens or lawful permanent residents
- All owners with 20% or more equity interest must provide a personal guarantee
- Owners with 20%+ equity who have been indicted, convicted, on parole, or on probation for a felony are ineligible
- Business must be for-profit (non-profit organizations are ineligible)
