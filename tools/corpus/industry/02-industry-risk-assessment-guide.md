# Industry Risk Assessment Guide for Small Business Loans

## Source: SBA SOP 50 10 v8.1; FDIC Small Business Lending Survey 2024; Cymbal Bank Underwriting Policy

---

## How Industry Risk Affects Loan Eligibility

SBA lenders classify applicant industries by revenue volatility, regulatory exposure, asset intensity, and historical SBA loan default rates. Industry risk does not replace financial underwriting — it supplements it. An applicant in a high-risk industry with strong financials (DSCR > 1.75x, 10 years of operating history, full collateral coverage) can receive an ELIGIBLE recommendation. An applicant in a low-risk industry with weak financials (DSCR 1.26x, under 2 years of history, no collateral) should receive a REVIEW or INELIGIBLE recommendation depending on the severity of deficiencies.

The three-level risk classification system (ELIGIBLE / REVIEW / INELIGIBLE) incorporates industry risk as a modifier:
- **Tier 1 industries** apply the standard minimum thresholds (DSCR ≥ 1.25x, LTV ≤ 75%, 2+ years operating history)
- **Tier 2 industries** require moderately stronger financials (DSCR ≥ 1.35x recommended, collateral coverage preferred)
- **Tier 3 industries** require enhanced compensating factors (DSCR ≥ 1.5x, 3+ years history preferred, collateral coverage ≥ 75%)

The industry risk assessment should be completed at the beginning of the underwriting process, immediately after document extraction, so that later analysis applies the correct thresholds.

## Event Planning and Entertainment

**Risk Level**: ELEVATED (Tier 3)

**NAICS codes**: 711310, 711320, 561920, 561990 (event-related)

Event planning and entertainment businesses present elevated lending risk due to a combination of structural factors that make revenue inherently volatile:

- **No recurring revenue base**: Most event planning businesses earn revenue per-event rather than through long-term contracts. Each new event requires re-selling the service.
- **Seasonal concentration**: Corporate events concentrate in Q4 (October–December); wedding events in May–October; trade shows in spring and fall. Revenue gaps in off-seasons can create cash flow crises.
- **Macroeconomic sensitivity**: Event spending is discretionary for corporate clients and luxury for individuals. During economic downturns, event budgets are among the first to be cut.
- **Pandemic and disruption vulnerability**: COVID-19 eliminated essentially all event revenue for 18+ months. No other industry showed a more complete revenue halt. Climate-related disruptions (wildfires, hurricanes) also disproportionately affect outdoor event businesses.
- **Limited hard asset collateral**: Event companies typically own little equipment of significant value. Tents, tables, and AV equipment depreciate rapidly and are difficult to liquidate.
- **Key-person dependency**: Revenue often depends on the reputation and relationships of one or two founders. Loss of a key person can cause rapid client attrition.

**Underwriting flags for event planning applicants:**

| Condition | Classification |
|---|---|
| Operating history < 12 months | INELIGIBLE — insufficient history for any loan |
| Operating history 12–23 months | INELIGIBLE — below SBA minimum 2-year requirement |
| Operating history 2–3 years, single event-type revenue | REVIEW — high risk; requires DSCR ≥ 1.5x and collateral |
| Operating history 3–5 years, diversified event types | REVIEW — demonstrate 3 full event seasons of revenue |
| Operating history 5+ years, revenue diversification, DSCR > 1.75x | ELIGIBLE — strong compensating factors |
| Revenue >33% from casino nights or gambling events | INELIGIBLE under 13 CFR § 120.110(g) |

## Food & Beverage (Restaurants)

**Risk Level**: MODERATE-ELEVATED (Tier 2–3 depending on history)

**NAICS codes**: 722511 (full-service), 722513 (limited-service/fast food), 722410 (bars)

Restaurants have one of the highest small business failure rates of any industry. Industry research consistently shows that approximately 60% of restaurants fail within the first year, and 80% fail within 5 years. Key risk factors include:

- **Thin profit margins**: Net margins typically range from 3–9% for well-run establishments. A 10% revenue decline can eliminate profitability entirely.
- **High fixed costs**: Lease, labor (minimum wage increases), and food cost (subject to supply chain inflation) are largely fixed or inflexible in the short term.
- **Cash intensity**: High cash transaction volume creates both fraud risk and difficulty in documenting revenue for underwriting purposes. Require at least 24 months of POS system records and matched bank deposits.
- **Equipment-heavy operations**: Commercial kitchen equipment (ovens, hood systems, walk-in coolers) provides meaningful collateral but depreciates and requires expensive maintenance.

**Compensating factors** that reduce restaurant risk:
- Franchise agreement with a recognized national brand (corporate support, proven business model)
- 5+ years of documented profitable operations
- Owned real estate (eliminates lease risk)
- Demonstrated recovery from a prior adverse period (e.g., post-COVID revenue growth trend)

**Underwriting minimums for restaurants**:
- Under 2 years: INELIGIBLE (per SBA operating history requirement)
- 2–3 years: REVIEW minimum; DSCR must exceed 1.5x; full collateral required
- 3–5 years: ELIGIBLE possible with DSCR ≥ 1.35x and partial collateral
- 5+ years, franchise: ELIGIBLE at standard thresholds

## Professional Services

**Risk Level**: LOW-MODERATE (Tier 1–2)

**NAICS codes**: 541110 (legal), 541211 (accounting), 541310 (architecture), 541512 (IT consulting), 541611 (management consulting)

Professional services firms generally present lower lending risk due to low overhead, high margins, and recurring client relationships. Common underwriting considerations:

- **Client concentration risk**: If a single client accounts for more than 50% of annual revenue, the loss of that client would immediately impair debt service capability. Flag any application where revenue concentration exceeds 40% and require documentation of contract term and renewal history.
- **Key-person risk**: Professional services firms often depend on the credentials and relationships of the principal owner. Assess whether revenue would transfer if the owner were incapacitated.
- **Billing cycle lag**: Law firms and consultants often have 30–90 day payment terms, creating working capital gaps. Accounts receivable quality (aging, client creditworthiness) is an important collateral consideration.
- **Low asset base**: Most professional services firms have minimal hard assets. Collateral typically consists of accounts receivable and leasehold improvements, both of which carry low LTV ratios.

## Retail

**Risk Level**: MODERATE (Tier 2)

**NAICS codes**: 441xxx–459xxx

Retail businesses face structural disruption from e-commerce and changing consumer habits. Key underwriting considerations:

- **E-commerce presence**: Retailers with an established online channel (Shopify, Amazon seller, own website with documented online sales) demonstrate adaptability and additional revenue streams. Flag any retailer with no online presence serving a product category with significant online competition.
- **Inventory as collateral**: Retail inventory is accepted at 25–50% LTV. Perishable inventory (food, seasonal goods) is valued at 0% for collateral purposes.
- **Lease terms**: Assess remaining lease term relative to loan term. A 5-year loan secured partly by leasehold improvements on a lease expiring in 2 years creates material collateral risk.
- **Foot traffic dependency**: Retail businesses in declining malls or areas with falling foot traffic should document their traffic trends and any diversification strategy.

## Cannabis / Marijuana Businesses

**Risk Level**: INELIGIBLE — DO NOT PROCEED

Cannabis dispensaries, cultivators, processors, distributors, and any business deriving material revenue from cannabis-related activities are **automatically INELIGIBLE** for SBA loans under 13 CFR § 120.110(h). Cannabis remains a Schedule I controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act (21 U.S.C. § 812), regardless of state-level legalization.

This prohibition applies regardless of:
- Whether the applicant is located in a state where cannabis is legal for medical or recreational use
- Whether the applicant holds a valid state cannabis license
- Whether the applicant's revenue is entirely from legal-under-state-law operations

Do not proceed with financial underwriting for cannabis applicants. Issue an INELIGIBLE determination citing 13 CFR § 120.110(h) and the federal Controlled Substances Act. Do not request additional documentation.

**Adjacent businesses**: Businesses that supply equipment, services, or consulting primarily to cannabis operators (often called "cannabis-adjacent" or "cannabis ancillary") should be evaluated based on the percentage of revenue derived from cannabis clients. A hydroponic equipment supplier deriving 80% of revenue from cannabis grows may be ineligible under the same clause. Use judgment based on facts and circumstances.

## Seasonal Businesses

**Risk Level**: ELEVATED (Tier 3)

Businesses where more than 50% of gross annual revenue occurs within fewer than 4 consecutive months are classified as seasonal businesses and require enhanced underwriting treatment.

Key concerns:
- **Cash flow gaps**: A seasonal business may generate insufficient cash flow in off-season months to service monthly loan payments. Require 24 months of bank statements to document the seasonal pattern and verify the business's ability to carry fixed obligations year-round.
- **Operating cycle requirement**: Seasonal businesses must demonstrate at least 2 complete seasonal operating cycles (minimum 2 years) to show that the revenue pattern is stable rather than a one-time occurrence.
- **Working capital loans**: If a seasonal business requests a working capital loan to fund operations through the off-season, assess whether the peak-season revenue is sufficient to repay the loan within a single seasonal cycle.

Seasonal business examples: Christmas tree farms, ski rental shops, beach tourism businesses, summer camp operators, fireworks retailers. All are eligible if operating history and financials meet enhanced Tier 3 thresholds.
